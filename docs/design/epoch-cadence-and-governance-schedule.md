# Epoch Cadence and Governance Schedule

## About Epochs

City/Sync uses fixed **3-month Epochs** to establish a predictable cadence for governance and system learning.

Each Epoch has a defined start and end date. At the end of every Epoch, the system can recalibrate:
- Issuance caps
- Redemption rates for current offerings
- Addition or removal of offerings
- New MCE proposal and voting cycle parameters

Why this cadence:
- It creates enough operating time to observe behavior and outcomes.
- It prevents constant rule churn that can confuse participants and partners.
- It gives Issuers and Redeemers a reliable planning horizon.

---

## Standard Epoch Timeline (12 Weeks)

> Note: A 3-month Epoch maps to roughly 12 weeks.  
> This schedule assumes a full 12-week operating window.

### Week 1 (Day 1)
**Open voting begins** for current MCE proposals.

Why:
- Starts the Epoch with clear participant direction.
- Encourages immediate civic participation and signal generation.

### Weeks 1-4
Open voting remains active; participants can still change votes.

Why:
- Allows decision quality to improve as new information appears.
- Reduces premature lock-in.

### Weeks 4-5
Redeemers can adjust rates for currently active offerings.

Why:
- Lets Redeemers self-correct if redemptions exceed operational capacity.
- Improves supply/demand balance before the next reset period.

### End of Week 4
Open voting closes; vote changes are no longer allowed.  
Closed voting period begins.

Why:
- Establishes a hard cutoff for tally integrity.
- Prevents late-stage volatility and manipulation concerns.

### Weeks 5-6
Closed voting and validation period.

Why:
- Provides administrative time for final checks and process confidence.

### Week 6
Closed voting ends; MCE winner is selected.  
Open call for new proposals by Issuers and Redeemers begins.

Why:
- Transitions directly from decision to next-cycle proposal pipeline.
- Maintains continuity between epochs.

### Week 7
MCE planning phase begins.

Why:
- Converts winning intent into executable workstreams.
- Allows constraints and dependencies to be resolved before task issuance.

### Week 10
MCE tasks are finalized and created.  
Open call for Issuer/Redeemer proposals closes.

Why:
- Locks scope in time for launch readiness.
- Prevents planning drift late in the cycle.

### 2 Days Before Next Epoch
Issuer Committee convenes and finalizes the top 5 MCE proposals for the next Epoch ballot.

Why:
- Ensures a vetted, decision-ready slate is available at Epoch start.
- Preserves governance quality while keeping momentum.

### Day 1 of Next Epoch
MCE tasks are distributed and available to claim.

Why:
- Minimizes lag between planning and execution.
- Keeps community engagement high and predictable.

---

## Standing Rule During Every Epoch

Throughout the Epoch, Redeemers may commit offerings for any of the five active MCE proposals currently in vote circulation.

Why:
- Provides real-time private-sector signal on practical support.
- Helps participants and Issuers assess proposal viability beyond votes alone.
