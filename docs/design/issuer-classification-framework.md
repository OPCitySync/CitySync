# Issuer Classification Framework

Purpose:
- classify issuer organizations to improve onboarding quality, anticipate operational risk, and preserve task governance integrity.
- provide a shared method for pilot decisions now and committee-led governance later.

Use cases:
- issuer onboarding decisions (approve, conditional approve, defer)
- task catalog quality control
- verification quality assurance
- issuance policy and calibration guidance

---

## 1) Two-Layer Classification Model

Classify each issuer with:
1. Function Type (what public-service mission they execute)
2. Operational Profile (how reliably they can run task lifecycle responsibilities)

This separates mission relevance from execution reliability.

---

## 2) Function Type Taxonomy

### A) Community Service Issuers
Examples:
- food security organizations
- community support nonprofits
- neighborhood service programs

Governance significance:
- high local impact, often broad participant accessibility.

### B) Public Agency-Adjacent Issuers
Examples:
- library programs
- parks/community center programming
- publicly affiliated service initiatives

Governance significance:
- strong institutional legitimacy and verification pathways.

### C) Specialized Issuers
Examples:
- environmental restoration groups
- elder-support initiatives
- education/tutoring networks

Governance significance:
- may require tighter credentialing and safety controls.

### D) MCE Coordination Issuers
Examples:
- organizations leading task execution for MCE priorities

Governance significance:
- high cross-organization coordination burden and timeline sensitivity.

---

## 3) Operational Profile Dimensions (Scored 1-5)

Score each issuer on each dimension.

1. Mission Alignment Strength
- How clearly do tasks align with public-good/public-service outcomes?

2. Volunteer Operations Maturity
- Existing capacity to recruit, schedule, and supervise volunteers.

3. Task Design Legibility
- Ability to define clear task scope, one-hour valuation basis, and success criteria.

4. Verification Reliability
- Consistency and quality of completion review and evidence handling.

5. Safety and Credential Controls
- Adequacy of risk controls for high-sensitivity tasks.

6. Policy Discipline
- Compliance with task rules, catalog governance, and epoch constraints.

7. Equity and Accessibility Coverage
- Ability to issue tasks that are inclusive across participant constraints.

8. Data and Reporting Readiness
- Ability to provide reliable lifecycle metrics and governance feedback data.

9. Recovery Responsiveness
- Speed and quality of correction when task quality or process issues emerge.

---

## 4) Risk Tiering

Aggregate profile into tier outcomes.

### Tier G (Green): High-Fit / Low-Risk
- strong mission fit and operational reliability across lifecycle stages.

Policy default:
- standard onboarding and periodic review cadence.

### Tier Y (Yellow): Medium-Fit / Managed-Risk
- mission fit is strong but one or more process controls are weak.

Policy default:
- conditional onboarding with remediation milestones and tighter review intervals.

### Tier R (Red): High-Risk / Deferred
- repeated rule violations, unstable verification quality, or inability to manage tasks safely.

Policy default:
- defer onboarding or pause issuer status until controls are demonstrated.

---

## 5) Behavior Anticipation by Profile Pattern

If Task Design Legibility is low and Verification Reliability is low:
- expect high dispute/noise rates and participant trust erosion.
- mitigation: mandatory template coaching and pre-issuance review gate.

If Policy Discipline is low and Volunteer Operations Maturity is low:
- expect inconsistent issuance and higher no-show/rejection volatility.
- mitigation: capped issuance permissions, staged expansion, additional oversight.

If Safety/Credential Controls are weak for specialized tasks:
- expect operational and legal risk concentration.
- mitigation: narrow allowed task scope until controls are validated.

If Equity Coverage is low:
- expect participation skew and reduced system legitimacy.
- mitigation: require diversified task formats and accessibility-focused catalog feedback.

---

## 6) Pilot Rules Gate (Pass/Fail Before Scoring)

Issuer task proposals must satisfy:
- tasks do not replace paid functions
- tasks deliver public good/public service outcomes
- tasks are verifiable through legible success/evidence criteria

Any issuer unable to maintain these conditions remains ineligible for full issuance status.

---

## 7) Public-Facing Narrative Mapping

For each accepted issuer class, communicate:
1. what public need this issuer type addresses
2. why this issuer type is operationally suited for task issuance
3. how City/Sync ensures task quality and participant fairness
4. how governance responds if issuer performance degrades

This keeps onboarding legitimacy understandable to residents and partners.

---

## 8) Pilot-to-Maturity Governance Path

### Pilot Phase
- City/Sync-led issuer classification with committee oversight.
- emphasis on reliability baselines and rule compliance.

### Transitional Phase
- joint City/Sync + committee scoring with published rationale summaries.

### Maturity Phase
- committee-led classification and calibration governance with shared standards oversight.
- cross-city comparability through common taxonomy and profile rubric.

---

## 9) Minimum Data Required per Issuer Profile

- organization type and mission category
- volunteer program baseline capacity
- task proposal volume and approval ratio
- verification turnaround quality metrics
- no-show/rejection incidence patterns
- safety/credential exception history
- participant feedback and issue-resolution data
- equity/access distribution notes

---

## 10) Decision Outputs (Standardized)

Each classification review should output:
- function type label
- profile scorecard (1-5 by dimension)
- risk tier (G/Y/R)
- onboarding decision (approve/conditional/defer)
- required controls and milestones (if conditional)
- next review date

This creates governance memory, comparability over time, and defensible onboarding decisions.
