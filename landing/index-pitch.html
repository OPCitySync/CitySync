Purpose

Build the missing coordination layer for public life.

Markets do not reliably price civic contribution, and traditional administrative systems cannot absorb local civic capacity at scale. City/Sync creates a repeatable mechanism to identify, coordinate, verify, and reward civic labor without replacing public institutions.

  • Make civic activity legible so it can be planned and improved.
  • Help institutions route community effort into high-need outcomes.
  • Create a common framework for replication across cities and programs.
Problem

Civic energy exists. Coordination infrastructure does not.

Cities are full of contribution, but the systems that govern service delivery still treat most civic labor as invisible or incidental. The result is fragmentation, low accountability, and missed public value.

01

Invisible Value

Non-market care and civic work are real inputs to city function, but they are rarely measured in operational systems.

02

Disconnected Capacity

Community effort is high but not routable through shared standards, making execution brittle and one-off.

03

Legacy Governance Friction

Top-down coordination alone struggles to adapt quickly to local context, urgency, and distributed contributors.

Solution

A shared loop that cities can govern and grow.

City/Sync introduces a role-based public-sector economy where issuer organizations post civic tasks, participants execute verified work, and redeemers provide local goods and services. Credits are minted by verified contribution and burned at redemption.

Issuers

Define, issue, and verify mission-aligned civic tasks.

Participants

Claim and execute work; earn CITY and VOTE through verified completion.

Redeemers

Honor offerings and burn CITY through redemption to close the loop.

Pilot Execution

Berkeley and Mexico City as proving grounds.

The pilot validates whether this mechanism can operate under real-world constraints, with real organizations, participants, and redemption behaviors. The goal is not a concept demo. The goal is a procedural artifact cities can actually adopt.

Berkeley, California Mexico City, CDMX
Phase 1: Establish Role Operations Onboard issuers, redeemers, and participants into a controlled operating loop.
Phase 2: Verify Throughput + Reliability Track issuance, verification quality, and redemption rhythm under live conditions.
Phase 3: Codify Replication Standards Package governance and process logic for broader rollout and dPAN expansion.
After the Pilot

From one pilot to a framework for decentralized public administration.

Once validated, the City/Sync mechanism can support local-chain implementations, domain-specific dPAN applications, and eventually a broader market for exporting tested civic models. The long-term output is a common framework that lets cities share methods, not just ideas.

  • Codified task and redemption standards with city-level adaptation.
  • Interoperable governance patterns across applications and institutions.
  • A transferable coordination stack for global public-service innovation.
